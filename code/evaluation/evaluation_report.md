## 2. How the task scores, and what the scorer assumes

What the task documents say (the only sources):

- `problem_statement.md`, section "Evaluation": `output.csv` is compared with hidden canonical answers; scoring considers
  "the correctness of `deletion_status`, identity resolution, discovered records and risks, cited evidence, policy
  decisions, and the completeness and order of `steps_json`". It asks this report for case-level status accuracy,
  step-level and field-level accuracy, sequence accuracy, at least three additional cases, and failures and improvements.
- `README.md`, sections "Output" and "Evaluation": the same list; the grader parses `steps_json` as JSON, key order and
  whitespace do not matter, **array order does**.
- Neither document gives a formula, weights, or says whether steps are matched as a set or by position.

So the formula is unknown. `code/evaluation/score.py` therefore reports every component separately instead of folding
them into one number, and every analysis in this report that compares behaviours uses that same scorer
(`score.score`, no second definition):

| metric | definition in `score.py` |
|---|---|
| case status accuracy | predicted `deletion_status` equals the golden one |
| step-level precision / recall / F1 | steps compared as a multiset of (phase, system_id, resource_id, operation, state, evidence_id); `step_no` ignored |
| step identification | the same with state and evidence_id left out |
| field-level accuracy | per field, over golden steps aligned by (phase, system_id, resource_id, operation); `step_no` is compared too |
| sequence accuracy | longest common subsequence of the step keys divided by the longer sequence, averaged over cases |
| positional match | step *i* of the prediction equals step *i* of the golden answer in all seven fields |
| exact cases | status and every step equal, in order, including `step_no` |

The positional metric exists because of one finding (section 6.3): leaving a step out shifts the number of every later
step, which a set-based metric does not see and a position- or sequence-based one does.

### 3.1 Unrecoverable samples (documented, not chased)

`case_04`, `case_18` and `case_20` need an identifier that no file under `dataset/` contains: it is not in the evidence,
the activity stream, the identity tables or the manifest (checked with a recursive search of the whole `dataset/` tree;
the only hit is `sample_output.csv`, which the pipeline never reads). Producing them would mean inventing an id.
The code does not do that: there is no `account_00N` naming convention anywhere in the source (a test enforces it) and
no lookup of golden answers. These three steps are therefore counted as missed, and the report shows the score both ways
where it matters (4 of the 193 golden steps are missing; 3 of them are these).

## 6. The two dataset-specific heuristics, honestly

Both exist because a deleted account and an anonymized analytics profile leave **no identifier** in the evidence, so the
evidence alone cannot say which resource belongs to a case. They exploit how this dataset was generated. They would not
exist in a real system and should not be mistaken for domain knowledge.

1. **`reported_status` against worker result (tiebreak).** SUCCESS goes with COMPLETE, PARTIAL with PARTIALLY_COMPLETE,
   FAILED with NOT_STARTED. It is applied only after evidence and elimination have failed to decide, only narrows a set
   that is still tied, is ignored if it would empty the set, and never overrides evidence (tests enforce all four).
2. **Worker cadence (anonymized analytics profiles).** The worker spaces the resources of one case by alternating gaps
   (390 s and 449 s in the success family, 1110 s and 1169 s in the failure family). The cadence is learned from directly
   linked siblings, and an analytics event must sit one to three gaps before the first sibling event.

Both are **switchable** (`python3 code/main.py --no-tiebreak --no-cadence`, `PlanOptions(use_tiebreak=False,
use_cadence=False)`), **counted** (every use is listed in `code/evaluation/run_report.md`) and **backtested** as below.
Effect of the switches on the 518 evaluation requests:

<!-- block:switches -->
| tiebreak | cadence | accounts guessed or omitted | analytics profiles guessed | steps that differ from the default run | verdicts that differ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| on | on | 118 | 24 | 0 cases | 0 |
| on | off | 118 | 43 | 6 cases | 0 |
| off | on | 229 | 24 | 153 cases | 0 |
| off | off | 229 | 43 | 155 cases | 0 |

Neither switch moves a verdict. The tiebreak matters a lot for steps (111 accounts move from guessed or omitted to
decided); the cadence matters little (19 analytics guesses avoided, 6 cases whose steps actually change).

### 6.1 Backtests (generated: `code/evaluation/residual_backtest.md`)

The method for accounts: take the 255 cases whose account is named by evidence, hide that link for the whole
`reported_at` cohort at once, recover it from the activity stream alone, compare. No golden answer is involved.

| decision | cases | correct |
|---|---|---|
| elimination (accounts owned by others or taken are removed, a unique survivor is accepted) | 45 | 45 (100%) |
| tiebreak resolved it | 82 | 82 (100%) |
| guess with 2 candidates left | 99 | 53 (54%) |
| guess with 3 candidates left | 23 | 8 (35%) |
| guess with 4 or more candidates left | 4 | 1 (25%) |

Cadence cannot be backtested the same way, and the reason is itself a finding: **no compliant analytics profile is ever
named by evidence** (0 cases), because an anonymized profile carries no identifier, so there is nothing to hide. What
exists instead:

- ground truth from the golden answers of the COMPLETE samples: 7 cases (2 with one candidate, 5 with two), **7 right**.
  The cadence decided 3 of them (two candidates narrowed to one); with the cadence off, the same 5 two-candidate cases
  are guessed, also correctly. Seven cases is too few to call this an accuracy rate; it shows no failure, nothing more.
- a premise check on evidence-linked resources: of 421 consecutive worker events of linked resources, 414 are spaced by
  one of four gaps (390, 449, 1110, 1169 s); 46 of 47 evidence-linked analytics profiles follow their predecessor by one of
  those gaps. This supports the premise (the worker uses an alternating gap that includes analytics) but is not an
  accuracy figure for the matching.
- things tried that did not help and were not adopted: a 60-second band around the nominal analytics offset changed
  nothing (cohort-mates share the band, which is exactly the ambiguity the cadence resolves); ordering by case number,
  `requested_at`, user number or e-mail does not predict the order of events (45 to 60% agreement, chance level).

### 6.3 Ambiguity policy under the sample scorer

`omit` leaves the account step out, `guess` always picks, `hybrid` guesses only when two or fewer candidates remain.
On the 32 samples (criterion fixed in advance: step F1, then exact cases, then fewer extra steps):
omit 0.9842 / 26 exact, **hybrid 0.9895 / 28 exact**, guess 0.9869 / 28 exact. The samples contain only 3 ambiguous
accounts, so this is a thin margin. The larger check uses the same scorer on 199 evidence-established cases (the
account step is hidden, the case re-planned under each policy, and compared with the plan that has the true account):

Pseudo-golden = our own plan with the true account, 199 evidence-established cases whose account is verified absent (56 more are left out because the account is still present: a residual, unobserved account is never one), scored with `score.score` (the sample scorer). Only the account step can differ.

| policy | status acc | step P / R / F1 | field step_no | field resource_id | seq overlap | positional | exact cases |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| omit | 199/199 | 1.000 / 0.926 / 0.9614 | 0.715 | 0.926 | 0.894 | 0.715 | 105/199 |
| hybrid | 199/199 | 0.977 / 0.961 / 0.9685 | 0.918 | 0.961 | 0.941 | 0.918 | 149/199 |
| guess | 199/199 | 0.967 / 0.965 / 0.9660 | 0.964 | 0.965 | 0.947 | 0.964 | 155/199 |
<!-- /block:policy -->

The ranking depends on the metric: **hybrid wins on step F1**, **guess wins on every position-sensitive metric**
(`step_no`, sequence, positional, exact cases), and omit loses on those because a missing step renumbers everything after
it. Which family the hidden grader weighs more is not documented. The default stays `hybrid` (the approved choice); the
decision to switch to `guess` is the owner's.

## 7. Does the identity guesswork change a verdict? (no)

Of the 118 evaluation requests whose account is guessed, omitted or unmatched, **none** has a verdict that depends on
which candidate is chosen: every candidate has no admissible observation, so it is verified absent whichever it is
(`code/tests/test_options.py` checks that omit, hybrid and guess give identical verdicts for all cases). The residual
accounts affect exactly one step per case (the `VERIFY_RECORD_ABSENT` of the account): at most 108 steps of 3201
(3.4%), and by the backtest about 29 omitted plus roughly 36 wrong guesses.

The other 10 of the 118 ("unmatched") are 10 of the 12 image/PDF cases. They have no worker event the matcher can use,
**and** their verdict depends on screenshot facts: my unverified reading is that 8 of the 12 image/PDF cases are INCOMPLETE
where the current answer says COMPLETE. That is the largest verdict risk left
and sets the priority order: image/PDF reader first, identity guesses last.

## 8. Open risks

1. **A support id typed two ways (8 cases).** In 8 evaluation cases the home file (ticket with requester details)
   and a later-captured partner file (an attachment record, dated at or before the audit) describe the same support id
   differently. The clock rules make the later file identity-only, so the answer is the ticket (`REPORT_EXPOSED_PII`,
   redact). If the truth is an attachment (delete) or both, each case loses or gains three steps (24 steps, 0.7% of the
   total). No sample covers it. In the samples, every finding in a golden answer cites a file captured before the audit (75 of 75
   cited steps), which supports the current rule but does not prove it for this case.
2. **Audit records.** See section 9.
3. **Hero cases.** Eight of the 12 image/PDF cases have their audit record in evidence captured months after the audit
   (`evidence_01`, `evidence_02`). The planner's identity window (14 days before, 8 days after) does not reach it. Every
   one of the 22 samples whose audit record is visible anywhere has it in the golden answer, so a wider, audit-only window
   is justified; it is queued with the image/PDF work in Sprint 4.
4. **Rarely occurring semantics without sample truth:** legal-hold records (8 observations), tombstones (10), marketing
   profiles (1), `DELETION_PENDING` accounts (8 mentions), an expired export (1 case). `system_id` for object-storage,
   marketing and legal records is also unconfirmed. Counts in `coverage_report.md`, section 5.
5. **Row order** is natural numeric (`case_33` before `case_100`), matching `deletion_requests.csv` and the sample file;
   the brief only says "sorted by `case_id`".

## 9. The audit-record gap (83 evaluation cases)

What the plan outputs now: for the 93 cases (83 evaluation, 10 samples) in which no file names the user's audit record, **no
audit step at all**. No placeholder or guessed id is emitted. Of the 83 evaluation cases, 81 are INCOMPLETE and 2 are COMPLETE;
they sit in 14 batches of the typed-inventory, JSON-readback and lookup-trace families, where the home file lists audit
records only for other users.

Pattern in the 32 samples:

- 24 golden answers contain an audit step, 8 do not.
- The 8 without are exactly the 8 early single-scenario samples (cases 01, 02, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08 and 10), each with its own
  one-off home file (failover replica, search primary, attachment PDF, export jobs, support replica), and none of them has an
  audit record visible anywhere.
- All 24 samples from the shared batch families have an audit step in the golden answer (24/24), 22 of them with the record
  visible in the evidence and 2 (`case_18`, `case_20`, in a lookup-trace batch) with an id that no file contains.

Reading: when the user's audit record is visible, the golden answer has it (22/22); in the batch families it is present even
when the evidence lost it (2/2). The 83 cases are all in batch families, so the likeliest case is that their golden answers
contain an audit step that cannot be recovered. **Expected impact if so:** 83 missing steps, one per case, which is 2.6% of
the 3201 evaluation steps (recall only; no verdict changes). If their golden answers omit it, the impact is zero. A made-up id
would be a wrong step as well (one false positive plus the same missing step), so nothing is emitted.
